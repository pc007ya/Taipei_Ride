### Fixed high-load stress comparison

Same world/poses/camera sequence, **ultra**, **1920×1080 CSS**, actual DPR/scale **2**, verified **3840×2160 drawing buffer** on every measured frame. Three repetitions, 20 warm-up/120 measured frames, with rotated version order. All cases still report approximately **60 FPS / 16.67 ms**.

| Version | Static GPU ms | Moving GPU ms | Static submit ms | Moving submit ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Previous sample | 5.214 | 5.044 | 2.735 | 3.044 |
| Optimized sample | 5.301 | 5.280 | 2.674 | 3.038 |
| Main | 4.001 | 3.973 | 2.160 | 2.258 |

The optimized sample's high-load GPU mean is **1.7% slower static / 4.7% slower moving**, despite lower mesh, material, triangle and buffer costs. Static GPU repeat ranges overlap (previous 4.810–5.483 ms; optimized 5.092–5.542 ms). Moving ranges are previous 4.996–5.103 ms and optimized 5.118–5.506 ms. This stress result **does not demonstrate a GPU improvement**; it is retained as a limitation, not hidden behind the low-quality result or the 60 FPS ceiling. Main remains cheaper at both budgets. No claim is made that this art sample is faster than main.

An intermediate candidate initially omitted only the low-amplitude character normals and architectural roughness maps. Its first unbalanced-order 4K trial did not improve GPU time (static 4.848→5.236 ms; moving 5.128→5.095 ms). The final candidate also removes asphalt micro-normals, retains its aggregate color texture, and removes unused mounted-character skin buffers. The final balanced trials above still show the stated high-load limitation.

Normal application-loop keyboard walking and stationary six-second windows also measured about 60 FPS for all three versions at low quality, **1440×960 CSS / scale 0.75**, not the controlled 0.55 budget. Native riding/walking, braking and turning were exercised. Historical software-GPU numbers around 2.33/2.24 FPS for the old sample and 4.34/4.24 for main remain distinct historical results, not a hardware comparison or evidence that the sample beats main.

The CPU profile covers startup, warm-up and measurement: shader/program compilation and initial traffic mesh baking are substantial bursts. Steady rendering includes scene traversal, matrix updates, frustum tests and draw submission. At low quality GPU elapsed time is below submission wall time; at the 4K ultra budget GPU time exceeds submission wall time. Both fit inside the 16.67 ms cadence; no saturated steady-state CPU/GPU bottleneck is demonstrated on this Mac. Full `.cpuprofile` and raw frame data are retained locally in `qa/`; CPU profiling timing is not mixed into the three-run performance averages.

## Cost and visual changes

| Asset | Draw meshes before → after | Triangles before → after | Geometry bytes before → after | Texture objects before → after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Walking character | 12 → 8 | 28,438 → 24,022 | 1,398,540 → 886,628 | 8 → 5 |
| Complete scooter/rider | 20 → 16 | 45,110 → 36,470 | 2,999,052 → 959,276 | 12 → 7 |
| Three-building street | 14 → 14 | 19,668 → 19,668 | 2,596,176 → 1,694,088 | 23 → 13 |

These are factory-visible mesh counts, not total scene draw calls. Final measured static scene draws decrease **147→143**, triangles **269,798→262,262**; moving final frame draws **170→164**, triangles **324,444→310,668**. Shadow passes explain differences from the per-asset budgets.

- Anatomical body, fingers, clothing topology, skin weights, rider contacts and colliders remain unchanged. A regression compares every source anatomical triangle index against the factory geometry.
- Sphere accessories use 12×8 instead of 24×16 segments; scooter curved shells use 14 instead of 28 longitudinal segments. Small shoes/eyes and near-inspection curves can show more faceting. Helmet and major rider anatomy remain detailed.
- Each shoe is one indexed matte draw per moving foot, preserving sole/upper/lace colors with vertex colors. Fine upper fabric texture and separate lace roughness are removed; foot attachment and motion remain unchanged.
- Low-amplitude normal maps are removed from skin, clothing, leather and paint; color texture patterns and hair normal detail remain. Glossy paint/lens/helmet clearcoat and reflections remain.
- Asphalt micro-normal noise is removed while its color grain remains. Building roughness becomes scalar rather than noise-textured. Tile/paving/concrete/wood/metal color and normal maps, shop signs, glass, weathering and interior layers remain. Repeated concrete uses cached texture objects.
- Mounted static body meshes omit unused skin-index/weight buffers. Indexed merging retains source vertex indices for accessories/vehicle parts. Street indexing welds only vertices with identical position, normal, UV and color, retaining hard edges and texture seams. No triangle is removed from the architecture.

Same-camera before/previous/after screenshots were visually inspected for walker, scooter side and daytime street; the automated set additionally checks mounted rider. Raw screenshots and reports stay locally under `qa/hardware-results` and `qa/hardware-report`. The hardware JSON summaries and repeatable model-budget script are committed.
